# M² analysis of the fibre speckle image sets

Generated by `m2_analysis.py`. Calibration used: **λ = 532 nm**, **10 mm** camera translation per frame, **3.45 µm** pixel pitch.

Beam widths are ISO 11146 second-moment (D4σ) diameters, computed after border-ring background subtraction and integrated over an elliptical aperture of 3× the D4σ diameter, iterated to convergence. M² comes from the hyperbolic fit d²(z) = a + bz + cz², M² = (π/8λ)·√(4ac − b²).

> M² scales linearly with the assumed stage step and inversely with the assumed wavelength. If either constant is wrong, every number below moves by the same factor and all *relative* comparisons still hold.

## Conclusion

**Baseline beam quality scales with core size.** With no magnets the 50 µm fibre gives M² = 15.3 and the 105 µm fibre M² = 42.3, a ratio of 2.76. For a fully filled step-index fibre M² grows in proportion to core diameter × NA, so a ratio of 2.10 would be expected if both were filled identically; the measured ratio being larger indicates the 105 µm fibre is excited over a wider mode set (more overfilled) than the 50 µm one.

**50 µm fibre.** Baseline M² = 15.34 ± 0.20. Normal: 25 magnets → M² = 15.20 (-0.9%, not significant), 50 magnets → M² = 15.67 (+2.2%, not significant). Alternating: 25 magnets → M² = 15.58 (+1.6%, not significant), 50 magnets → M² = 14.40 (-6.1%, significant). At 50 magnets the two configurations differ by 1.27 in M² (Normal higher), against a combined standard error of 0.27 — a significant difference, so Normal degrades the beam measurably more than Alternating in this fibre.

**105 µm fibre.** Baseline M² = 42.33 ± 0.25. Normal: 25 magnets → M² = 41.62 (-1.7%, not significant), 50 magnets → M² = 41.37 (-2.3%, significant). Alternating: 25 magnets → M² = 40.82 (-3.6%, significant), 50 magnets → M² = 40.90 (-3.4%, significant). At 50 magnets the two configurations differ by only 0.47 in M², against a combined standard error of 0.50 — not significant, so on this data the two configurations cannot be distinguished for this fibre.

**Direction of the effect.** Every change that clears the noise is a *reduction* in M² — the magnets make the beam slightly better, not worse, by up to 6.1%. That rules out simple scattering into higher-order modes, which would raise M². A reduction instead points to the magnets acting as a weak mode filter: bending or stressing the fibre strips the highest-order modes, which are the least well confined and contribute most to the second moment. Note the effect is small and could also be a systematic of the launch drifting slightly when the magnets are fitted, so it is worth confirming with a repeat in which the magnets are added and removed several times in the same session.

**Which fibre is more sensitive to the magnets.** The largest magnet-induced change in M² is 6.1% for the 50 µm fibre against 3.6% for the 105 µm fibre, so in *relative* terms the 50 µm fibre responds more even though its baseline M² is lower. The 105 µm fibre is the more consistent of the two, though: 3 of its 4 magnet conditions move significantly and they all move the same way, whereas the 50 µm fibre has 1.

**Overall.** Of the 8 magnet conditions measured (2 fibres × 2 configurations × 2 magnet counts), 4 differ significantly from their own 0-magnet baseline. The magnets change M² by at most a few percent in either direction, which is small compared with the ~3× difference between the two fibres. On this data set the magnet configuration is a second-order effect on beam quality: the core size dominates.

**Caveat on the absolute scale.** Every M² above is proportional to the assumed z-step and inversely proportional to the assumed wavelength; the comparisons between fibres, configurations and magnet counts are unaffected because those constants are common to all scans.

## Data quality flags

64 of the step-index scans do not meet the full ISO 11146 sampling recommendation (≥10 points, ≥5 within one Rayleigh length of the waist and ≥5 beyond two Rayleigh lengths). In these scans the waist sits near the start of the travel, so the near-field side is under-sampled and the fitted M² carries more uncertainty than the bootstrap error alone suggests. See `per_scan_fits.csv` for the per-scan flags.

- Frames containing a secondary bright lobe that was masked out before the moment integration: **320** of 1641
- Frames rejected as aspect-ratio outliers (x/y width ratio more than 1.35× away from their own scan's median): **0** (0.0%)
- Further frames rejected by the >3σ residual cut in the caustic fit: **74** (4.5%)
  Both are flagged per frame in `per_image_widths.csv` (`aspect_outlier`, `used_in_fit_x`, `used_in_fit_y`) and drawn as ✕ on the caustic plots, so every rejection is auditable.
- Saturated frames (peak = 255): **4**
- Frames where the beam was large enough that the integration aperture had to shrink to its floor of 1.6×D4σ to stay on the sensor: **147**. A tighter aperture truncates the outer wings, which biases the width slightly low; these are all at the far end of the travel, where the beam is widest. `aperture_factor` in `per_image_widths.csv` gives the value used for every frame.
- Scans analysed: **98** (64 in the step-index campaign, the rest are earlier/legacy runs kept in the CSVs)
